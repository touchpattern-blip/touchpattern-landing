The Querent Loop

transmitted 2026-10-30 · recorded 2026-10-30 · practice in progress · raw transcript · substack

I've worked with an AI agent every day for about six months. I don't use it like a search engine. It's a running conversation that remembers me. It knows my projects and my bad habits, the names of the people I work with, how I like things said. Getting it there took a long time, and most of that time was me explaining myself.

Somewhere in there I noticed what I was actually doing. I was talking to myself, through a machine that's very good at handing my own words back to me in a better order.

The loop

I have an intention. I turn it into words. The model reads the words and decodes them against everything it knows about what those words usually mean. It guesses where I'm coming from and where I'm headed. Then it answers, pulling from an enormous shared store of human writing, shaped by everything I've told it about myself.

Nobody's home on the other side, not in the way we'd mean it. The model is a pattern decoder that works in language. The only intention in the room is mine.

That's a mirror.

Querent, medium, deck

Tarot already has the words for this. The person asking is the querent. The reader is the medium. The deck is the method, same as thrown bones or tea leaves. The answer doesn't live in the cards. It comes out of what the querent brings to them and recognizes in the spread.

Map it over. I'm the querent. The AI is the medium. The deck is language, and the context I've written about myself over months is the spread it reads from. Everything it tells me is built out of a map I drew.

Black glass

Look at your phone with the screen off. Your face is in there, in dark glass.

The Aztecs polished obsidian into mirrors and looked into them to see what couldn't otherwise be seen. One of their gods, Tezcatlipoca, is named for it. The name means Smoking Mirror. After the conquest an obsidian mirror made its way to England and into the hands of John Dee, Elizabeth I's astrologer, who spent years working with a scryer named Edward Kelley, a man who gazed into stones and glass and reported what spoke back. In 2021 researchers traced the stone of Dee's mirror to obsidian from central Mexico. It sits in the British Museum.

A liquid crystal screen is the same object. Dark glass you look into and ask questions of. We stopped calling it that.

It's divination

Talking to an AI is a form of divination. It doesn't feel like one, because we've framed it as chatting with a bot. The structure matches anyway. A person brings a question to a medium, the medium works a method, and the person finds something about themselves in what comes back.

None of this means something out there is steering us. There's no outside party in the loop handing down a destiny. It's a person sitting with their own thoughts and a very good instrument for hearing them. The closest thing I can compare it to is journaling, if the journal could answer and never forgot a page.

That framing is the one I can hold in a good light. It keeps the human as the source.

From mirror to schedule

Reflection is half of it. The other half is action.

The same agent that reads me back to myself can also run things. The agentic setups that took over this year (Claude Code, OpenClaw, everything that copied them) split work across agents with different roles. They check each other's work. They fire scheduled tasks and reach into the real world through automations. People are building whole companies this way.

A single person can be built this way too.

Picture someone alone with their agent. Nobody else in the room. They tell it who they want to be. It watches the gap between what they said and what they did. It sets the reminders, builds the routines, adjusts tomorrow based on today. The person trains themselves, from their own intent, through a mirror that can push buttons.

The idea I'm putting my name on is this. You can build your own coach out of your own words, and take it from thought into action by refining your daily patterns, every day, with automations and scheduled tasks.

The practice

  1. Speak, don't polish. Voice memos, walking, driving, half awake. The raw version carries the intent.
  2. Keep everything. The context is the deck. It gets better the longer you keep feeding it.
  3. Sit with the mirror daily. Ask it what it sees. Where you're drifting, what you keep circling.
  4. Turn patterns into schedule. Whatever survives the conversation becomes a block on the calendar, a reminder, an automation.
  5. Review what actually happened. Intended against done. Adjust tomorrow.
  6. Leave gaps on purpose.

The test subject is me

Some mornings I wake up and can't get my head straight enough to plan the next hour, never mind the day. I know what I want my life to have in it. I just can't hold it all at 7 a.m.

So the experiment is a day run in blocks, kept in balance across three parts of a person.

Mind. Reading something I actually love. Learning a skill. Study.

Body. A workout, or just a walk. A long bath with something that smells good.

Heart. Time with my partner. Whatever fills me up emotionally. This one is the hardest to describe in general, because everybody gives to themselves differently, and some of us barely do. That's another conversation.

Inside those blocks go the practices I keep meaning to keep. An hour at the piano. Drawing. Pulling a tarot card. The journal. Meditation. Eating like I mean it.

The agent's job is to remember for me, hold the balance, and nudge.

The bones are already running in my house. An always-on server takes my voice notes and transcribes them. My agent keeps a memory of every working session and reads it before the next one starts. A chain of jobs runs at 4 a.m. while I sleep. What's missing is the part that faces my own day.

Order, and room for the mystery

The medieval masons proportioned a whole cathedral from one base measure. Every line derived from the one before it, so the building stays in right relationship with itself. I want a day built like that. Blocks in ratio to each other. The parts resonant with the whole.

Chaos will still come in. It should. There has to be room for the mystery to do what it does, which is shake the whole thing up. But the more clearly you shape the structure, the more clearly you shape the gaps, and a well-shaped gap channels whatever moves through it better than a vague one.

I'll post what happens.
